💪 Why Track Your Workouts?

The Science of Progressive Overload

Tracking your workouts is one of the most powerful habits you can build as a gym-goer. When you record your sets, reps, and weights, you create a clear picture of your progress over time. It helps you avoid plateaus by knowing exactly when to increase the load, and it keeps you accountable on days when motivation is low.

Studies show that people who track their training consistently make significantly more strength and muscle gains compared to those who train without any record. Whether you are a beginner or an experienced lifter, having a logbook makes a measurable difference in your results.

🏋️ Beginner Tips for the Gym

Key Principles for Safe Training

Start Light: Focus on learning correct form before adding heavier resistance. Good form builds a strong neuromuscular foundation and prevents joint strain.

Rest & Recovery: Muscles rebuild and grow during sleep and recovery periods. Allow at least 48 hours of recovery before training the same muscle group intensely again.

🥗 Nutrition & Gym Performance

Fueling Your Training & Recovery

Protein is essential for muscle tissue repair — aim for roughly 1.6 to 2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight. Hydration and complex carbohydrates provide glycogen stores for sustained energy during lifting sets.
